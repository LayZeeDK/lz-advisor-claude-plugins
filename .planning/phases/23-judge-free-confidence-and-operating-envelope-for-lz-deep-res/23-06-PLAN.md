<objective>
Capture the one fresh q2 pair, retain its evidence before anything can age out, and settle the ENV-05
spike against the frozen ceiling.

This plan spends the phase's largest metered budget and walks its sharpest one-way door: a capture
whose artifacts are not copied immediately cannot be re-derived without re-spending the capture. The
built-in q1 capture's session is already gone from the transcript store entirely, which is why the
retention protocol is frozen rather than advisory.

Purpose: the spike settles whether a verification-complete built-in report is reachable inside the
frozen ceiling, which gates ENV-06 and feeds ENV-07 either way. Its first observation also resolves
the one pre-committed conditional in the freeze -- whether the built-in's workers leave recoverable
fetched content -- and that answer selects between two branches both written before the spike ran.
Output: the q2 pair on disk with retained evidence and passing MANIFESTs, plus the committed spike
record.

**Two of Task 1's three pre-registered replies do not run the built-in capture, and the built-in capture
IS the spike.** Option B captures the lz side only; option C captures neither. On both, no spike is
dispatched, no spike record is written, and ENV-05 and ENV-06 terminate on branch (c)
NOT-ATTEMPTED-BY-BUDGET with the AMENDMENT RECORD as the reason -- which is what option B's own text
already says. Tasks 2 and 3 therefore key every step and every gate on the RECORDED option, tolerate an
absent capture rather than failing on it, and fabricate nothing to satisfy a check. What this plan owes
the downstream plans on those branches is a legible recorded descope in its SUMMARY, because that is
what they read where a spike record would otherwise be. It is never written as branch (b): branch (b)
claims a method was attempted and shown unable to deliver, and on those options nothing was attempted.

Metered session-pool spend, expected to span two reset windows. The go is a blocking human decision in
Task 1.
</objective>
